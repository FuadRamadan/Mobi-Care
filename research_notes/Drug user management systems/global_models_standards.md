## 1. Portugal's 2001 decriminalisation and the Commissions for the Dissuasion of Drug Addiction (CDTs)

### Takeaway
Portugal made personal possession (up to about 10 days' supply) an administrative offence. Police refer people to regional CDT panels of legal, health and social-work professionals, which mostly suspend cases and steer dependent users to treatment. Early outcomes were favourable: fewer drug deaths, less HIV, less prison burden, no big rise in use. These cannot be credited to decriminalisation alone, because treatment and harm-reduction investment rose at the same time. Since about 2019 the model has shown strain: rising overdoses in Lisbon, visible drug scenes in Porto, a caseload that is mostly cannabis, underfunded harm reduction, renewed punitiveness, and a 2024 institutional restructuring (SICAD replaced by ICAD).

### Cited Findings
- **Law:** in 2001 Portugal decriminalised possession of up to ten days' supply of all illicit drugs. Drugs stay illegal, but users get no criminal record and are referred to treatment — [Hughes & Stevens summary via UNSW newsroom](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2010/11/busting-drug-law-fears); [Transform Drug Policy Foundation, UNODC UNGASS contribution](https://www.unodc.org:443/documents/ungass2016/Contributions/Civil/Transform-Drug-Policy-Foundation/Drug-decriminalisation-in-Portugal.pdf)
- **Workflow:** police refer people found in possession to regional panels of legal, health and social-work professionals (the CDTs). Most referred cases are "suspended", which effectively means no penalty. Commissions can impose fines, suspend professional licences or require reporting — [Transform/UNODC](https://www.unodc.org:443/documents/ungass2016/Contributions/Civil/Transform-Drug-Policy-Foundation/Drug-decriminalisation-in-Portugal.pdf); [MSU International Law Review blog](https://www.msuilr.org/new-blog/2020/1/27/portugals-drug-policy-what-are-the-effects-of-a-decriminalization-framework)
- **Outcomes (Hughes & Stevens 2010, the first independent academic evaluation):** more investment in treatment and harm reduction, such as methadone. A modest rise in adult-reported use, no bigger than in other southern European countries. Lower use among school pupils, fewer drug-related deaths, less HIV/AIDS, and a smaller burden of drug offenders on prisons — [UNSW newsroom 2010](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2010/11/busting-drug-law-fears); [Citywide Ireland summary](https://www.citywide.ie/decriminalisation/impacts/improved-health-outcomes/)
- **Attribution caveat:** a 2014 UK Home Office review said the outcomes could not be credited to decriminalisation alone and were probably influenced by expanded treatment and harm reduction — [Citywide / search summary of Home Office 2014](https://www.citywide.ie/decriminalisation/impacts/improved-health-outcomes/)
- **Recent challenges:**
  - 2008 law changes, a more diverse drug market and a more visible drug scene have increased policing of drug use and the criminal sanctions that follow. Continued benefits "require ongoing investment in treatment and health supports" — [UNSW DPMP Drug Summit 2024 explainer](https://unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/ada/sprc/research-reports/dpmp-drug-summit-2024-explainers/2024-11-was-decriminalisation-effective-in-portugal-v3.pdf)
  - Academic analysis describes a "sharp increase of punitiveness targeted at drug users" over the past decade despite decriminalisation — [Int J Drug Policy / PMC8285857](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8285857/)
  - **Caseload drift:** citing SICAD's 2022 data, 71% of CDT administrative offences involved cannabis, and 73% of the cannabis users were assessed as non-problematic. The commissions now mostly handle non-problematic cannabis use rather than the heroin users they were designed for — [TalkingDrugs, "An eternal pilot?"](https://www.talkingdrugs.org/an-eternal-pilot-is-portugals-decriminalisation-system-frozen-in-time/)
  - **Re-criminalisation above the threshold:** the crime of consumption has been re-established for quantities above the legal threshold (the abstract does not say how; [unverified background] it was a 2008 Supreme Court ruling), and commission practice contradicts itself — [Lisbon Addictions 2022 abstract](https://2022.lisbonaddictions.eu/node/5304.html)
  - **Funding:** most harm-reduction services are run by NGOs, some funded by government only once every two years. Lisbon and Porto each had only one consumption room. In January 2024 SICAD was restructured into ICAD (Institute for Addictive Behaviours and Dependencies) — [TalkingDrugs](https://www.talkingdrugs.org/an-eternal-pilot-is-portugals-decriminalisation-system-frozen-in-time/)
  - Overdose rates hit 12-year highs and nearly doubled in Lisbon between 2019 and 2023. In Porto, collection of drug-related street debris rose 24% and crime rose 14% between 2021 and 2022 — [Washington Post via Press Herald, July 2023](https://www.pressherald.com/2023/07/08/once-hailed-for-decriminalizing-drugs-portugal-is-now-having-doubts/)
  - **Counterpoint:** Portugal still has one of the lowest overdose death rates in the developed world — [Cato, Singer 2023](https://clips.cato.org/sites/default/files/Singer_WP_Portugal.pdf)

### Inferences
- What does the work in the Portuguese model is the health-led referral pathway backed by funded treatment, not decriminalisation on its own. A national system that copies the CDT front end without funding treatment and harm reduction risks the stagnation now seen in Portugal.
- CDT data shows a design risk: a diversion body that screens everyone who is caught fills up with low-risk cannabis cases. Triage, with fast-track dismissal for non-problematic use and intensive follow-up only for dependence, saves capacity.
- [Unverified background] CDT records are kept separately from criminal records, and SICAD/ICAD reports CDT data (processes, rulings, substances) annually. A comparable system should keep diversion records administrative, time-limited and away from police databases.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 ICAD evaluation or current CDT statistics were found (number of processes, share referred to treatment).
- Full outcome numbers (HIV notifications, prison populations over time) could not be pulled from primary papers because PMC and UNSW were blocked.
- No reliable current information was found on whether Portugal has changed the CDT model or thresholds in 2025–2026.

## 2. EUDA/EMCDDA Treatment Demand Indicator (TDI) protocol: fields and anonymised unique identifiers

### Takeaway
The TDI (Standard Protocol 3.0) is the EU standard for reporting people **entering** drug treatment. It is one of the five EMCDDA/EUDA key epidemiological indicators. It defines a common set of data items and recommends avoiding double counting within and across treatment centres and regions. The ideal is a central national register with a unique identifier per client, typically pseudonymised (for example, an algorithm built from name and date of birth). The protocol accepts that some countries cannot do this for legal, administrative or economic reasons.

### Cited Findings
- TDI 3.0 is the EMCDDA's guideline for reporting data on people entering drug treatment. It defines each data item, revises version 2.0, and is one of five key epidemiological indicators — [EMCDDA TDI Protocol 3.0 (OFDT copy)](https://en.ofdt.fr/sites/ofdt/files/2024-05/emcdda-tdi-protocol-3.0_392671.pdf); [EUDA manual page](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/manuals/tdi-protocol-3.0_en)
- **Double counting:** the protocol says double counting should be avoided wherever possible, including across regions and between treatment centres. It describes a central national register with a unique identifier per client as the ideal, and accepts that some countries cannot adopt one for administrative, economic or legal reasons — [EMCDDA TDI Protocol 3.0 PDF](https://www.euda.europa.eu/system/files/publications/675/EMCDDA-TDI-Protocol-3.0_392671.pdf)
- **Identifier construction:** identifier-based deduplication often uses an algorithm on name and date of birth, with more sophisticated options such as digital fingerprints. Double counting inflates the count of people treated — [EMCDDA TDI overview](https://www.euda.europa.eu/system/files/publications/568/EMCDDA-TDI-overview_155372.pdf)
- Without a deduplication mechanism such as a unique identifier, a client who enters treatment more than once in a year may be counted twice — [EUDA Statistical Bulletin, treatment demand](https://www.euda.europa.eu/data/stats2017/tdi)

### Inferences
- [Unverified background, to check against the protocol PDF] TDI 3.0 was published in 2012. Its data items cover:
  - **Treatment centre:** type of centre, treatment year, type of treatment (outpatient, inpatient, low-threshold, GP, prison).
  - **Treatment status:** ever treated before; referral source (self, family, GP, health or social services, court/police/probation, other).
  - **Demographics:** sex, age, nationality, living status (with whom and where), labour status, education.
  - **Drug use:** primary drug and secondary drugs, route of administration, frequency, age at first use.
  - **Risk behaviour:** ever injected, age at first injection, needle sharing, testing for HIV/HCV/HBV.
- [Unverified background] Countries build pseudonymous codes in several ways. France and Spain use hashed codes made from initials, date of birth and sex. Some countries (for example Italy, Czechia) use encrypted national ID numbers. Australia's SLK-581 (section 3) is a comparable non-EU example.
- For a national system: adopting TDI-compatible fields at intake allows international comparison (EUDA, UNODC ARQ module A06) at no extra cost. A one-way hashed pseudonym held separately from identifying data is the accepted pattern.

- The itemised TDI 3.0 field list and the exact identifier guidance could not be fetched (euda.europa.eu was blocked). The writer should treat the field list above as indicative until checked.
- No evidence was found of a TDI 4.0 revision as of 2026.

## 3. US TEDS and PDMPs; Australia AODTS-NMDS; UK NDTMS and the Treatment Outcomes Profile (TOP): data model, consent, outcomes reporting

### Takeaway
- **Episode-based or person-based:** TEDS (US) counts treatment **episodes**, not people. AODTS-NMDS (Australia) counts closed episodes and uses a statistical linkage key (SLK-581) to count clients. NDTMS (England) is person-level and identifiable to the national body, with patient consent, and it collects TOP outcome measures at start, every six months and at exit.
- **PDMPs** are a separate model: prescription surveillance. Mandated prescriber checks reduce doctor-shopping and risky opioid prescribing, and interstate hubs (RxCheck, PMP InterConnect) connect the states. Evidence of an effect on overdose deaths is mixed.

### Cited Findings
**US TEDS**
- TEDS is a repository of treatment data routinely collected by states. It covers admissions to, and discharges from, facilities licensed or certified by the state substance abuse agency, mainly those receiving state alcohol and drug agency funds — [SAMHSA TEDS-A 2023 codebook](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/system/files/media-puf-file/teds-a-2023-ds0001-info-codebook_v1.pdf)
- TEDS records represent treatment episodes, not individuals. The admissions minimum data set has 19 items. Discharge records (TEDS-D) add type of service at discharge, length of stay and reason for discharge — [SAMHSA TEDS-A 2023 codebook](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/system/files/media-puf-file/teds-a-2023-ds0001-info-codebook_v1.pdf); [TEDS-D 2023 codebook](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/system/files/media-puf-file/teds-d-2023-ds0001-info-codebook_v1.pdf)
- The 2023 TEDS annual report was published on 29 August 2025, a roughly two-year lag — [SAMHSA 2023 TEDS report](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/report/2023-teds-annual-report)

**US PDMPs**
- **Mandates:** query volume rose sharply after Kentucky's 2012 mandate, with similar jumps in Tennessee, New York and Ohio. In Kentucky and Ohio, multiple-provider episodes, opioid prescribing, overlapping opioid prescriptions and overlapping opioid/benzodiazepine prescriptions all fell — [Penn LDI review](https://ldi.upenn.edu/our-work/research-updates/prescription-drug-monitoring-programs-evolution-and-evidence/); [OJP abstract](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/effects-mandatory-prescription-drug-monitoring-program-pdmp-use)
- In Medicare, the share of enrollees getting prescriptions from five or more doctors was 8% lower in mandate states. An earlier 34-state study found an average reduction of 1.12 prescription-opioid overdose deaths per 100,000 in the year after implementation — [Penn LDI](https://ldi.upenn.edu/our-work/research-updates/prescription-drug-monitoring-programs-evolution-and-evidence/)
- Limited use mandates showed no reduction in prescription stimulant or depressant quantities — [PMC10334646](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10334646/). Commentators question whether PDMPs push patients toward illicit supply — [NEJM-indexed commentary, PMC7486903](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7486903/)
- **Interstate sharing:** federal overdose-prevention funds are conditioned on joining RxCheck. PMP InterConnect (run by the National Association of Boards of Pharmacy, NABP) links clinicians in about 47 states. Both hubs use end-to-end encryption — [Connecticut PMP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/prescription-monitoring-program/prescription-monitoring-program); [Penn LDI](https://ldi.upenn.edu/our-work/research-updates/prescription-drug-monitoring-programs-evolution-and-evidence/)

**Australia AODTS-NMDS**
- **2023–24 collection:** the search extract of this summary reads "107 establishments, 22,104 closed treatment episodes, 13,682 clients aged 10+", down from 22,393 episodes in 2022–23. These look like state-level (Queensland) or truncated figures, because national AODTS-NMDS totals are normally in the hundreds of thousands. Do not use them as national totals until checked against AIHW tables — [QNADA summary](https://qnada.org.au/2023-24-alcohol-and-other-drug-treatment-services-national-minimum-data-set/)
- **Closed episodes:** an episode has defined start and end dates. It closes when treatment ends, after three months without contact, or when the main treatment type, principal drug of concern or delivery setting changes — [AIHW AODTS-NMDS Data Collection Manual 2023–24](https://www.aihw.gov.au/getmedia/284ba49b-4e2d-403b-a294-fa6cd1318fdd/aodts-nmds-data-collection-manual-2023-24_v2.pdf)
- **SLK-581:** format XXXXXDDMMYYYYX, built from letters of the family name and given name, date of birth and sex. It lets records for the same person be linked without names. Client counts (available since 2013–14) use only records with a valid SLK-581 — [AIHW manual](https://www.aihw.gov.au/getmedia/284ba49b-4e2d-403b-a294-fa6cd1318fdd/aodts-nmds-data-collection-manual-2023-24_v2.pdf); [AIHW early insights](https://www.aihw.gov.au/getmedia/e873d52c-37e1-4af2-8120-680321040010/aihw-hse242-alcohol-and-other-drug-treatment-services-in-australia-early-insights_1.pdf)

**UK NDTMS / TOP**
- **Consent:** the NDTMS patient information and consent form explains that treatment data is shared with OHID. Clients can withdraw consent at any time, and OHID states: "We will not share information about you with the police, social services or the courts" — [NDTMS patient information and consent form](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/62fbac198fa8f504bbbe7fc8/NDTMS-patient-information-and-consent-form.pdf)
- **TOP collection:** the keyworker completes the TOP with the client at treatment start, at each six-month review and at exit. It covers days of use of each substance in the past 28 days, injecting behaviour, and days worked or in education, among other items. A secure-estate version is completed within two weeks of reception — [NDTMS adult community combined review form (CDS-P)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5e6f772b86650c727b2f474b/NDTMS_adult_community_combined_review_form_CDS-P_Print.pdf); [secure estate TOP form](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5e6f766486650c7272f4c5dc/NDTMS_secure_estate_TOP_form_CDS-P_Print.pdf)
- NDTMS is defined through versioned "core data sets" (CDS-N, O, P, Q and later), with technical definitions, CSV headers and validation rules. Criminal justice intervention teams have their own data set that includes TOP — [CJIT technical definitions CDS-N](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/669990/Criminal_justice_intervention_team__CJIT__technical_definitions__core_dataset_N.pdf); [CDS-Q consultation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/609d33c5d3bf7f2888d19031/NDTMS_Core_Dataset_Q_Consultation.pdf)
- **England 2024–25 (OHID, published 4 Dec 2025):**
  - 329,646 adults were in contact with treatment, up 6% on the previous year and the highest figure on record.
  - 169,542 were new entrants.
  - Opiate clients numbered 138,255 (42%); alcohol-only clients numbered 99,955 (30%).
  - Secure settings: 52,401 adults in treatment (published 5 Feb 2026).
  - Sources: [OHID adult statistics 2024–25](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/substance-misuse-treatment-for-adults-statistics-2024-to-2025/adult-substance-misuse-treatment-statistics-2024-to-2025-report); [secure settings 2024–25](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/substance-misuse-treatment-in-secure-settings-2024-to-2025/alcohol-and-drug-treatment-in-secure-settings-2024-to-2025-report)

### Inferences
- There are three tested architectures:
  - **TEDS:** episode-level, de-identified, funding-linked state reporting. Simple to run, but it cannot count people or track outcomes.
  - **AODTS-NMDS:** episode-level with a statistical linkage key. Gives client counts while staying pseudonymous.
  - **NDTMS:** person-level and consented, with repeated outcome measurement (TOP). Allows outcome reporting such as completion, re-presentation and abstinence change.
- A national system that wants outcome monitoring needs NDTMS-style repeated measures. It should also copy NDTMS's explicit firewall from police and courts, because that firewall underpins trust and voluntary engagement.
- [Unverified background] NDTMS identifies clients by initials, date of birth, sex and local authority (an "attributor"). It links to ONS mortality data and, for research, to Police National Computer data under data-sharing agreements. TEDS public-use files are de-identified, and US 42 CFR Part 2 tightly restricts disclosure of substance-use treatment records.
- PDMPs are useful for diversion of prescription medicines (opioids, benzodiazepines, and pregabalin or tramadol where relevant) but not for illicit-market drugs. Mandates without treatment capacity risk pushing patients toward illicit supply.

- Adult successful-completion rates and TOP outcome figures for 2024–25 were not captured; the full GOV.UK report could not be fetched.
- The AODTS-NMDS 2023–24 headline numbers need checking against AIHW tables (the search extract appeared truncated).
- No verified source was found for the current NDTMS legal basis (consent versus statutory or public-task basis under UK GDPR). The consent form indicates consent-based sharing.
- No 2025–2026 PDMP evaluation was captured.

## 4. UNODC/WHO standards and programmes: International Standards (2020), alternatives to conviction or punishment, drug treatment courts, TREATNET, DATIS/ARQ, mhGAP

### Takeaway
UNODC and WHO set the normative framework. Treatment should be voluntary, evidence-based, ethical and health-centred. For people in contact with the criminal justice system, treatment should be offered as an alternative to conviction or punishment at every stage (arrest, prosecution, trial, sentencing, post-sentence), based on assessment, with informed consent, proportionality and procedural safeguards. Treatment must not be forced. Drug treatment courts reduce reoffending on average. Global data flows through the UNODC Annual Report Questionnaire (ARQ), revised in 2020, which includes a treatment module (A06).

### Cited Findings
- The 2020 UNODC-WHO International Standards for the Treatment of Drug Use Disorders include section 5.3 on drug use disorders among people in contact with the criminal justice system. The draft (E/CN.7/2020/CRP.6) says effective treatment should be available as a partial or complete alternative to conviction and should not be forced on people against their will — [UNODC-WHO International Standards, March 2020](https://www.unodc.org/documents/drug-prevention-and-treatment/UNODC-WHO_International_Treatment_Standards_March_2020.pdf)
- **UNODC implementation principles:**
  - Drug use disorders call for health-centred responses: treatment rather than punishment.
  - Alternatives apply at every stage of the criminal justice process, based on assessment.
  - Diversion requires proportionality, informed consent, legal and procedural safeguards, and attention to special groups.
  - Source: [UNODC ATI Implementation Principles (2026)](https://www.unodc.org/documents/drug-prevention-and-treatment/UNODC_ATI_Principles_2026.pdf)
- The UNODC-WHO handbook "Treatment and care for people with drug use disorders in contact with the criminal justice system: alternatives to conviction or punishment" (2019) says such measures apply to personal-use offences and minor offences as defined in national law — [UNODC/WHO ATI handbook](https://www.unodc.org/documents/drug-treatment/UNODC_WHO_Alternatives_to_conviction_or_punishment_EN_0919.pdf). A self-paced ATI e-learning course was launched in November 2024 — [UNODC news](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/drug-prevention-and-treatment/news-and-events/2024/November/ati-e-learning.html)
- The Council of Europe Pompidou Group has issued standards for treating drug use disorders in custodial settings — [CoE standards](https://rm.coe.int/stantards-for-treatment-of-people-with-drug-use-disorders-in-custodial/1680a9b0cd)
- **Drug treatment courts:** a 2012 Campbell systematic review (Mitchell et al.) pooled 154 evaluations: 92 adult, 34 juvenile and 28 DWI courts.
  - Adult courts: recidivism fell from about 50% to about 38%, and the effect lasted at least three years.
  - Juvenile courts: smaller effects.
  - DWI courts: not consistently effective in randomised trials.
  - Sources: [Mitchell et al., Campbell Systematic Reviews 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:camsys:v:8:y:2012:i:1:p:i-87); [Tübingen copy](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/10900/64686/1/Mitchell_DrugCourts_Review.pdf)
- An earlier meta-analysis of 54 studies found a roughly 14% reduction in recidivism and did not analyse cost-effectiveness — [Justice Canada](https://justice.gc.ca/eng/rp-pr/csj-sjc/jsp-sjp/rr06_7/p4.html)
- **ARQ:** the revised ARQ was endorsed by the CND in 2020 and is sent to Member States every year. For 2023 data, 133 of 200 potential respondents reported. Relevant modules: A03 people who inject drugs, A04 people with drug use disorders, A05 drug-related mortality, A06 drug-related treatment (coverage, patient entry type and pathway) — [World Drug Report 2025 Methodological Annex](https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/WDR_2025/Annex/WDR2025_Methodological_Annex.pdf); [OAS/CICAD ARQ treatment data presentation](https://www.oas.org/ext/en/main/calendar/event/moduleid/7596/id/884/lang/1/controller/item/action/eventdownload)
- **Treatment gap:** only about one in five people with drug use disorders were in treatment in 2021 — [UNODC World Drug Report 2023](https://www.unodc.org/res/WDR-2023/WDR23_Exsum_fin_DP.pdf)

### Inferences
- A national system's diversion module should line up with the ATI principles:
  - assessment-based eligibility;
  - informed consent, with the option to decline and go through the ordinary process;
  - proportionality, so that treatment conditions are no more burdensome than the sanction they replace;
  - data protection;
  - no compulsory detention.
- [Unverified background] **UNODC TREATNET:** a network and training package (TREATNET Quality Standards; Treatnet Training Package Volumes A–C) covering screening, assessment, treatment planning, and community and family approaches.
- [Unverified background] **DATIS (Drug Abuse Treatment Information System):** UNODC/Colombo Plan-era regional treatment-data systems (for example in South and Southeast Asia and Africa, such as SENDU in Kenya). They are generally the ancestors of TDI-like national treatment registries.
- [Unverified background] **WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide 2.0 (2016, updated 2023 guideline):** includes a "Disorders due to substance use" (SUB) module for non-specialist primary care, covering ASSIST/AUDIT screening, brief intervention, withdrawal management and referral.

- TREATNET, DATIS and mhGAP details were not verified by search this session.
- No UNODC-specific evaluation of drug treatment courts outside the US was found.
- The final published 2020 Standards text (as opposed to the CND draft) was not fetched.

## 5. Screening and assessment tools commonly embedded in systems (ASSIST, AUDIT, DAST, ASI, CIWA), urine drug testing and outcome measures

### Takeaway
Systems typically layer a brief screen (ASSIST, AUDIT or AUDIT-C, DAST-10) with a structured severity assessment (ASI) and condition-specific clinical scales (CIWA-Ar for alcohol withdrawal; COWS for opioid withdrawal). Outcomes are tracked with repeated brief measures such as the TOP in the UK, or the Australian Treatment Outcomes Profile (ATOP). Validated cut-offs vary by population, so local validation matters.

### Cited Findings
- **DAST-10 scoring:** 0 = no problems; 1–2 low (monitor); 3–5 moderate; 6–8 substantial; 9–10 severe — [DAST-10 (NIDA CDE)](https://cde.nida.nih.gov/system/files/DrugAbuseScreeningTest_2014Mar24.pdf); [Pitt PA-SBIRT DAST-10](https://pasbirt.pharmacy.pitt.edu/pluginfile.php/270/mod_page/content/41/Screening%20Tools-DAST-10.pdf)
- **DAST psychometrics:** good internal consistency and temporal stability, and it identifies people who need fuller assessment. In one study, cut-off 4 gave sensitivity 0.98 and specificity 0.91 — [Yudko et al. psychometric review, PubMed 17306727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17306727/)
- **ASSIST validation (WHO; Australian validation study):** concurrent validity shown through correlations with ASI-lite, SDS, AUDIT and DAST. ROC analysis set cut-offs with suitable sensitivity and specificity for most substances — [Drug and Alcohol Review, ASSIST validation](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09595230500170266)
- The ASI is "perhaps the most widely used structured interview" for assessing use and impairment across drugs and alcohol — [Miller assessment guide, Guilford](https://guilford.com/add/miller11_old/assess_guide.pdf)
- **TOP fields:** days of use per substance in the past 28 days, injecting, and work or education days, collected at start, 6 months and exit — [NDTMS review form](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5e6f772b86650c727b2f474b/NDTMS_adult_community_combined_review_form_CDS-P_Print.pdf)
- **Test strips and new substances:** UNODC warns that fentanyl or nitazene test strips may fail to detect newer nitazene structural variants. This limits point-of-care drug testing for emerging synthetic opioids — [UNODC EWA news, Feb 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/e69b2ff5-5b91-4eea-8e1f-802ca7ad5080)

### Inferences
- [Unverified background] Standard facts on each tool:
  - **ASSIST v3.1:** 8 questions per substance; risk bands for alcohol of 0–10 low, 11–26 moderate, 27+ high; for other substances 0–3, 4–26, 27+.
  - **AUDIT:** 10 items; 8+ indicates hazardous drinking.
  - **CIWA-Ar:** 10 items, maximum 67; under 8–10 minimal withdrawal, 20+ severe.
  - **COWS:** 11 items, used for opioid withdrawal and induction.
- [Unverified background] Typical urine detection windows:
  - amphetamines and cocaine metabolite (benzoylecgonine): about 2–4 days;
  - opiates: about 1–3 days;
  - cannabis: several days with occasional use, up to 30 days or more with heavy use;
  - methadone: up to about a week.
  - Standard immunoassays do not detect nitazenes or synthetic cannabinoids; these need LC-MS. Guidance (for example SAMHSA TIP 63, and UK clinical guidelines known as the "Orange Book") supports testing as a clinical aid, not a punitive tool.
- In a criminal-justice diversion context, testing results should drive clinical review rather than automatic sanctions, in line with the ATI proportionality principle.

### Gaps
- CIWA-Ar, COWS and urine-testing detection windows were not verified from a primary source this session.
- WHO ASSIST manual cut-offs were not fetched.
- No source was found comparing which tools specific national systems mandate (beyond TOP in England).

## 6. Early warning systems for new psychoactive substances (UNODC EWA, EU EWS): relevance to nitazenes and kush

### Takeaway
The EU Early Warning System, run by EUDA with Europol, and the UNODC Early Warning Advisory (EWA) are the two main international NPS surveillance systems. They collect forensic and toxicology notifications, run risk assessments and feed scheduling decisions. Nitazenes are now the leading emerging synthetic-opioid threat: 34 analogues had been notified to UNODC in at least 37 countries by February 2026. Sierra Leone's "kush" has been shown to contain nitazenes (about half of samples) or synthetic cannabinoids. A national system needs a forensic and toxicology alert loop linked to treatment and harm-reduction services.

### Cited Findings
- **EU EWS:** formally notified its 1,000th substance in 2024; 47 new substances were identified in 2024, including emerging nitazenes and semi-synthetic cannabinoids — [EUDA poster: EU EWS monitors 1000 NPS](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/poster/eu-early-warning-system-monitors-1000-nps_en); [EU EWS topic overview](https://euda.europa.eu/archived/publications/topic-overviews/eu-early-warning-system_sk)
- **UNODC EWA:**
  - 101 newly emerged NPS reported in 2024, led by semi-synthetic cannabinoids and synthetic opioids — [UNODC EWA Sept 2025](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/b3a9f989-a42e-4c65-b462-c283ebc89e8b)
  - 34 nitazene analogues notified since the first in 2019, detected in at least 37 countries; test strips may miss newer variants — [UNODC EWA Feb 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/e69b2ff5-5b91-4eea-8e1f-802ca7ad5080)
  - Earlier count: 26 analogues in 30 countries in February 2025 — [UNODC EWA Feb 2025](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/b47cf39e-f557-4001-98a8-536af5673e9e)
- **Scheduling:**
  - The CND placed four more nitazenes under international control (Schedule I, 1961 Convention), in force June 2025, with ten nitazenes controlled since 2021 at that point — [UNODC EWA June 2025](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/16bcac76-5c96-49f6-9d8a-56d1aa76cd7a)
  - The UK introduced a generic Class A definition for nitazenes on 15 January 2025, and China adopted class-wide control in mid-2025 — [UNODC EWA news](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/16bcac76-5c96-49f6-9d8a-56d1aa76cd7a)
  - WHO's 48th Expert Committee on Drug Dependence (October 2025) critically reviewed two nitazenes and one synthetic cannabinoid — [UNODC EWA Aug 2025](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/a50a10a4-919d-4db9-8164-b6ab063f80e9)
- **World Drug Report 2025:** 28 countries had reported nitazenes to the EWA, 12 of which are under international control. Nitazene deaths rose in Estonia and Latvia, and there were poisoning outbreaks from nitazenes sold as heroin in France and Ireland — [WDR 2025 Key Findings](https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/WDR_2025/WDR25_B1_Key_findings.pdf)
- **Current NPS Threats (8th report, Dec 2025):** nitazenes and brorphine analogues dominated newly emerged synthetic opioids — [UNODC Dec 2025](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/c0e64537-e486-49ec-8b87-2c23a68f51b5)
- **Kush (Sierra Leone/West Africa):**
  - GI-TOC testing (reported February 2025) found the common kush varieties contain nitazenes (nearly 50% of samples) or synthetic cannabinoids. This is the first documented spread of nitazenes into West African drug markets, and one detected nitazene is about 25 times more potent than fentanyl.
  - Sources named: China, the Netherlands and probably the UK; precursors are bought online and mixed in Freetown.
  - Nitazenes were first detected by the EU EWS in 2019.
  - Sources: [GI-TOC, Kush in Sierra Leone](https://globalinitiative.net/analysis/kush-in-sierra-leone/); [AP via WSLS, 25 Feb 2025](https://www.wsls.com/health/2025/02/25/the-main-ingredients-in-sierra-leones-kush-are-synthetic-opioids-and-cannabinoids-report-finds/)
- UNODC and Brazil launched a first study on nitazenes in Brazil in 2025 — [UNODC Brazil](https://www.unodc.org/cofrb/en/noticias/2025/1/unodc-and-the-government-of-brazil-launch-first-ever-study-on-the-presence-and-risks-of-nitazenes-in-the-country.html)

### Inferences
- A national drug-user management system should include:
  - a national EWS node that receives forensic lab, toxicology (post-mortem and emergency department) and drug-checking results;
  - a route to share them with the UNODC EWA (and the EU EWS where applicable);
  - an alert channel to treatment and harm-reduction providers and to emergency departments (naloxone stocking and dosing advice, since nitazenes may need repeated doses);
  - surveillance fields at treatment intake, for example primary substance coded as "kush/synthetic mixture, unknown", that can later be reclassified as lab data arrives.
- Because standard urine immunoassays and many test strips miss nitazenes and synthetic cannabinoids, the system needs access to reference-lab LC-MS capacity. Clinical records should log "suspected" versus "confirmed" substance.

### Gaps
- No EUDA 2025 EWS annual figures were captured (only the 2024 milestone).
- No EU EWS or UNODC analysis specific to kush in Sierra Leone was found beyond GI-TOC.
- The mechanics of the UNODC EWA (national focal points, data-entry portal) were not verified this session.

## 7. Human-rights criticisms of drug-user registries and compulsory detention, and the UN joint statement: pitfalls to avoid

### Takeaway
Compulsory registration and detention models have documented serious harms:
- **Russia:** the "narkouchet" registry.
- **Thailand:** registration plus police surveillance.
- **Indonesia:** compulsory rehabilitation through Integrated Assessment Teams.
- **China, Vietnam, Cambodia, Lao PDR:** compulsory detention centres.
- **Philippines:** barangay watch lists under Oplan Tokhang.

The harms include deterring people from care, long-lasting stigma, loss of jobs, licences and child custody, data leaks to police, forced labour and torture in detention, and, in the Philippines, lists that preceded extrajudicial killings. As a result, Duterte was arrested and surrendered to the ICC in March 2025. Twelve UN agencies (2012) and then thirteen (2020) called for compulsory drug detention centres to be closed and replaced with voluntary, community-based services.

### Cited Findings
- **UN Joint Statement (March 2012):** 12 UN entities called on states to close compulsory drug detention and rehabilitation centres without delay and release those held. It notes detention without due process, legal safeguards or judicial review, and recommends voluntary, evidence-informed, rights-based community health and social services — [UN Joint Statement JC2310 (UN Digital Library)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3950265/files/JC2310_Joint_Statement6March12FINAL_en.pdf); about 300,000 people were estimated to be held in such centres in Asia — [UNAIDS feature](https://unaids.org/ru/node/33935)
- **UN regional statement (1 June 2020):** 13 UN agencies in Asia-Pacific urged permanent closure, citing COVID-19 risks and recalling the 2012 statement — [UN Viet Nam: UN Regional Statement June 2020](https://vietnam.un.org/sites/default/files/2020-06/UNRegionalStatement1June2020_EN.pdf); [IDPC](https://idpc.net/publications/2020/06/un-joint-statement-compulsory-drug-detention-and-rehabilitation-centres-in-asia-and-the-pacific-in-the-context-of-covid-19)
- **Compulsory detention in Asia:** HRW (2012) estimated that more than 350,000 people were held without due process in centres in China and Southeast Asia, facing abuse and forced labour, with reporting on Vietnam, China, Cambodia and Lao PDR — [HRW, Torture in the Name of Treatment](https://www.hrw.org/report/2012/07/24/torture-name-treatment/human-rights-abuses-vietnam-china-cambodia-and-lao-pdr); [HRW news](https://www.hrw.org/news/2012/07/24/drug-detention-centers-offer-torture-not-treatment)
- **Thailand:** registered drug users are kept under surveillance by police and anti-drug agencies and their information is shared. Fear of the registry keeps people away from free care. Registration can brand people for years, with loss of jobs, housing and child custody — [HRW, Drugs, punitive laws, policies and policing practices, and HIV/AIDS (2009)](https://www.hrw.org/news/2009/11/30/drugs-punitive-laws-policies-and-policing-practices-and-hiv/aids); [Open Society Foundations, Effects of Drug User Registration Laws (2009)](https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/uploads/def77bbe-43fd-46ad-9f91-b1b8bd26a221/drugreg_20091001.pdf)
- **Russia ("narkouchet"):**
  - The registry is a form of state control that restricts civil rights: no driver's licence, bans from certain jobs, and frequent police harassment.
  - Removal requires about five years of abstinence, and a single relapse restarts the clock.
  - HRW reported clinics leaking patient lists to law enforcement.
  - Russia's chief narcologist reportedly said most people leave the registry by dying, not recovering.
  - Sources: [Core.ac.uk Georgia–Russia comparative assessment](https://core.ac.uk/download/71347534.pdf); [openDemocracy](https://opendemocracy.net/od-russia/dmitry-lebedev/narkouchet-i-borba-s-nim); [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/1079330.html)
  - A 2018 Public Health Reviews article describes how punitive policy erodes narcologists' professional independence and ethics — [PMC5984458](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5984458); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/1b55281e3ea24378839eb2cbc3839625)
  - Note: much of this evidence dates from about 2007–2018; current rules are unverified.
- **Indonesia:**
  - The National Narcotics Board (BNN) pushed compulsory treatment and pledged to "rehabilitate" 100,000 users in 2015, doubling yearly. That target exceeded the Ministry of Health's estimate of about 74,000 people who inject drugs. Critics warned this endangered harm-reduction gains and invited corruption and abuse — [IDPC / The Conversation 2015](https://idpc.net/news/2015/07/forced-rehabilitation-of-drug-users-in-indonesia-not-a-solution)
  - Integrated Assessment Teams (TAT: Health Ministry, Law Ministry, police, BNN, prosecutors) have decided placement since a 2015 Joint Regulation — [Indonesian journal (Sukowati)](https://journal.sragenkab.go.id/index.php/sukowati/article/download/142/64); [Antara News](https://en.antaranews.com/news/350981)
  - Indonesia is listed among countries that send people who use drugs to compulsory treatment centres — [Human Rights Law Review 2022, "Treatment in Liberty"](https://academic.oup.com/hrlr/article/22/1/ngab022/6369597)
- **Philippines (Oplan Tokhang):**
  - Police went door to door and put people on drug watch lists; those listed were often killed later. Some people who signed up for treatment ended up on community watch lists and were then shot.
  - Police logged about 6,200 deaths; the ICC prosecutor has estimated up to 30,000.
  - Duterte was arrested in Manila on 11 March 2025 and transferred to The Hague, charged with murder as a crime against humanity.
  - At 2026 pre-trial hearings, prosecutors presented "high-value target" spreadsheets that police allegedly used in operations.
  - Sources: [Cebu Daily News](https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/628101/rodrigo-duterte-arrest-petition-cites-bato-dela-rosas-drug-war-role); [Asia Media Centre](https://asiamediacentre.org.nz/former-philippine-president-arrested-by-icc-for-deadly-war-on-drugs); [Daily Tribune, Feb 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/02/24/prosecutors-detail-drug-war-killings); [TV5](https://news.tv5.com.ph/search/read/icc-takes-duterte-into-custody-to-face-murder-charges-for-drug-war-killings)

### Inferences
Design pitfalls a national drug-user management system must avoid, and the safeguards implied:
1. **Registries as surveillance:** keep treatment data in the health system only. Adopt NDTMS-style rules: no sharing with police or courts, and statutory confidentiality like US 42 CFR Part 2.
2. **Permanent labelling:** set automatic retention and deletion limits. Records must not trigger loss of licences, jobs or custody without individual clinical assessment and due process.
3. **Coerced "treatment" and detention:** any criminal-justice pathway must meet the UNODC/WHO ATI criteria (informed consent, proportionality, judicial oversight, community-based care) and the 2012/2020 UN statements. No compulsory detention centres.
4. **Target-driven quotas** (as in Indonesia in 2015) distort practice. Use outcome indicators (TOP-type) instead of headcounts.
5. **Community watch lists** (as in the Philippines) can be lethal. Do not create community-generated lists of suspected users. Restrict access through role-based permissions and audit trails.
6. **Trust drives uptake:** registries deter care-seeking (Thailand, Russia). Pseudonymous identifiers (TDI hashed codes, SLK-581) let the system count people and avoid double counting without a named national register.

- No current (2025–2026) HRW or Harm Reduction International reports on Thailand's or Indonesia's registration or compulsory treatment were captured. Thailand's 2021 Narcotics Code and current Indonesian practice should be checked.
- Current Russian registry rules (driving and employment bans, the five-year removal period) were not verified for 2026.
- The outcome of Duterte's ICC confirmation-of-charges proceedings as of late 2026 was not established.
