# Drug-User / Drug-Demand-Reduction Information and Management Systems in Africa, with Lessons for Sierra Leone

Research date: 29 October 2026. Method note: every fact below came from web search results (titles and snippets of the cited pages). Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (pubmed, pih.org, talkingdrugs.org, researchsquare.com and thesierraleonetelegraph.com did not resolve), so I could not check figures against the full text. Treat single-source numbers as provisional.

## Q1. Nigeria (NDLEA): demand reduction, treatment centres, the 2018 national survey and any digital drug-user database

### Takeaway
Nigeria has the strongest prevalence baseline in West Africa: the 2018 NBS/UNODC/EU household survey, which estimated 14.3 million past-year users. NDLEA runs a network of counselling and rehabilitation centres and a toll-free line. NDLEA reports how many people it counselled or treated as cumulative totals in press statements. I found no public evidence of a national digital drug-user registry ("DUDIS" or anything similar).

### Cited Findings
- The National Drug Use Survey was released in January 2019. It found past-year drug use among 14.4% of people aged 15–64, about 14.3 million people. The National Bureau of Statistics ran it with UNODC and EU support — [UNODC](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/frontpage/2019/January/nigeria--eu-and-unodc-release-first-ever-national-drug-use-survey.html)
- Survey sample: 38,850 household respondents plus 9,344 high-risk drug users, covering all states. Cannabis was the most-used drug. 4.6 million people used prescription opioids (tramadol, codeine) non-medically. Prevalence was 21.8% among men and 7.0% among women. About 3 million people live with drug dependence — [UNODC](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/frontpage/2019/January/nigeria--eu-and-unodc-release-first-ever-national-drug-use-survey.html); [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/features/14-3m-nigerians-aged-15-to-64-years-abused-drugs-in-2018-says-unodc-report/)
- Africa Check notes that officials sometimes misquote the figure as "14.4 million currently on drugs". The survey's figure is 14.3 million past-year users — [Africa Check](https://www.africacheck.org/reports/how-many-nigerians-use-drugs/)
- NDLEA reports that 32,402 drug users were counselled and treated from January 2021 to March 2024 — [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/news/32402-drug-users-counselled-treated-in-3-years-nationwide-marwa/); [Gazette NG](https://gazettengr.com/ndlea-treated-counselled-over-32000-drug-addicts-in-three-years-buba-marwa/)
- Earlier NDLEA figures: about 8,000 people counselled or rehabilitated in 2021, and 11,523 from January 2021 to May 2022 — [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/ndlea-and-challenge-of-tackling-drug-menace-in-nigeria/)
- NDLEA runs a toll-free line through which drug users can seek treatment at its 26 treatment facilities nationwide — [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/news/32402-drug-users-counselled-treated-in-3-years-nationwide-marwa/) (the facility count comes from search snippets of NDLEA coverage)
- A 2026 report says NDLEA began nationwide training for more than 100 counsellors to standardise protocols across rehabilitation facilities. The date is uncertain because the page date may be a scrape artefact — [Gazette NG](https://gazettengr.com/?p=497569)

### Inferences
- NDLEA's treatment reporting appears to be an aggregate count kept by the enforcement agency, not a patient-level health record. That makes cohort outcomes such as retention and relapse invisible.
- The 2018 survey is now eight years old. Nigeria's value for Sierra Leone is as a model for a survey (household survey plus a high-risk-user module), not as current data.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of an NDLEA electronic drug-user database or a "DUDIS". Its existence is unconfirmed.
- No NDLEA demand-reduction budget figures and no 2025 or 2026 treatment totals.

## Q2. Kenya (NACADA): accreditation, helpline 1192 and data systems

### Takeaway
Kenya runs the most developed regulatory model for treatment facilities in the region: NACADA inspects facilities against national standards, accredits them and closes those that fail. It also runs a free 24/7 helpline, 1192, that refers callers to accredited facilities. Data rests on periodic national surveys plus the accreditation register.

### Cited Findings
- 1192 is a free, 24/7 NACADA helpline for telephone counselling, referral to accredited treatment facilities and incident reporting — [NACADA](https://www.nacada.go.ke/node/290); [NACADA Service Charter 2026](https://nacada.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-02/NACADA%20Service%20Charter.pdf)
- NACADA inspects treatment and rehabilitation facilities against the National Standards for Persons with Substance Use Disorders and issues accreditation certificates — [NACADA](https://nacada.go.ke/node/113)
- January 2026 Rapid Results Initiative: 236 facilities inspected across 36 counties. 135 were fully accredited, with about 3,800 residential beds between them. 56 have compliance gaps and remain under monitoring. 15 received immediate closure orders — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/nacada-closes-15-rehabilitation-facilities-for-health-standards-violations-n375800)
- Sources disagree on 30 other facilities. Citizen Digital says they were denied accreditation, while Nairobi Leo says they received conditional accreditation — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/nacada-closes-15-rehabilitation-facilities-for-health-standards-violations-n375800); [Nairobi Leo](https://nairobileo.co.ke/news/article/25153/alarm-as-report-reveals-over-13-million-kenyans-require-rehabilitation-for-drug-use)
- NACADA cites its latest national survey as showing that more than 1.3 million Kenyans need treatment and rehabilitation — [Nairobi Leo](https://nairobileo.co.ke/news/article/25153/alarm-as-report-reveals-over-13-million-kenyans-require-rehabilitation-for-drug-use)

### Inferences
- Kenya's approach rests on three parts: national standards, an accreditation register and a helpline that refers only to accredited facilities. Sierra Leone could copy this cheaply, especially as informal "detox houses" spread.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether NACADA has an electronic patient-level treatment information system, beyond the facility register and surveys.
- No NACADA budget figure or 1192 call-volume statistics found.

## Q3. South Africa: SACENDU, the CDA, the National Drug Master Plan and COSUP

### Takeaway
SACENDU, run by the South African Medical Research Council (SAMRC), is Africa's longest-running sentinel surveillance system for drug treatment. Specialist treatment centres submit admission forms, and SAMRC publishes results twice a year. The Central Drug Authority oversees the National Drug Master Plan but has weak control over its own budget. COSUP in Tshwane is the best-documented African harm-reduction programme that combines community-based opioid substitution therapy (OST) with needle and syringe services.

### Cited Findings
- SACENDU specialist treatment admissions were 7,244 in July–December 2024, 7,975 in January–June 2025 and 7,119 in July–December 2025 — [SAMRC Jan–Jun 2025](https://www.samrc.ac.za/sites/default/files/attachments/2026-02/Sacendu%20Research%20Update%20Jan-Jun%202025.pdf); [SAMRC Jul–Dec 2025](https://www.samrc.ac.za/sites/default/files/attachments/2026-09/Sacendu%20Research%20Update%20Jul-Dec%202025.pdf)
- SACENDU publishes a full annual report with a lag of about 18 months. Vol 26, published July 2025, covers January–December 2023 — [SAMRC](https://www.samrc.ac.za/sites/default/files/attachments/2025-08/SACENDU%20Full%20report%20Vol%2026%202025.pdf)
- Gauteng reportedly accounted for 51% of treatment admissions nationally. The reporting period is not confirmed — [Saturday Star via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/nb/stories/newspaper/Saturday-Star/GAUTENG-TOPS-NATION-IN-TEEN-SUBSTANCE-ABUSE-ADMISSIONS)
- The CDA is a statutory body established under Section 56 of the Prevention of and Treatment for Substance Abuse Act 70 of 2008. The NDMP 2019–2024 was launched on 26 June 2020 — [PMG CDA report](https://pmg.org.za/files/CDA_annual_report__1.pdf); [UCT Libraries](https://lib.uct.ac.za/government-publications/articles/2020-06-26-national-drug-master-plan-2019-2024)
- At a 2025 parliamentary briefing, the CDA reported that it does not control its budget, which the Department of Social Development administers. It also reported limited secretariat capacity and frequent leadership changes. A sixth draft of the next NDMP was ready for Cabinet — [PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/42649/)
- COSUP is implemented by the University of Pretoria and funded by the City of Tshwane, with the Gauteng health and social development departments as partners. From 2016 to 2019 it reached 1,513 adults (90% male, median age 30) through 17 sites, offering counselling, linkage to care and OST. It distributed about 17,000 needles a month. The programme treats substance use as a medical condition, not a crime — [PMC7284158](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7284158/)

### Inferences
- SACENDU shows that a low-cost sentinel system, built on a standard admission form and twice-yearly aggregation, can generate trend data for decades. This is a realistic first step for Sierra Leone's six or more treatment centres.
- COSUP shows that city-level, primary-care-based OST linked to HIV/TB services works. For Sierra Leone, nitazene-dominant kush makes opioid agonist therapy and naloxone directly relevant.

### Gaps
- I found nothing on South Africa's Department of Social Development treatment information system (the "TIMS" / Substance Abuse Treatment Information System). Its status is unverified.
- No current (2025–2026) COSUP client numbers found.

## Q4. Ghana, Liberia, Tanzania, Senegal, Mauritius and Côte d'Ivoire

### Takeaway
Ghana has the most progressive law in West Africa, Act 1019 of 2020, but implementation has stalled: the regulations and the rehabilitation fund the Act requires are still pending in 2026. Liberia is moving toward a public-health action plan but treats very few people. Tanzania and Senegal run the region's established methadone programmes, both using daily directly observed dosing.

### Cited Findings
**Ghana**
- Act 1019 provides for treatment, rehabilitation, harm reduction and alternatives to incarceration. In July 2026 the Deputy Interior Minister urged passage of the regulations that would make it operational. The Narcotics Control Commission (Drug Demand and Harm Reduction) Regulations, 2026 are with the Attorney General — [GNA](https://gna.org.gh/2026/07/interior-ministry-pushes-for-full-implementation-of-ghanas-drug-law/); [Ghanaian Times](https://ghanaiantimes.com.gh/interior-ministry-pushes-full-enforcement-of-drug-law/)
- The rehabilitation fund the Act requires had not been established as of August 2026. NACOC is pressing the government for it — [GNA](https://gna.org.gh/2026/08/nacoc-presses-government-for-rehabilitation-fund/)
- People are still being arrested for minor drug offences despite the Act. Introducing opioid agonist therapy (OAT) was on the agenda of the Second National Dialogue on Drug Policy in July 2026 — [MyJoyOnline](https://www.myjoyonline.com/nacoc-partners-hold-second-national-dialogue-on-drug-policy-reforms/); [IDPC](https://idpc.net/blog/2026/07/ghana-s-drug-policy-reform-must-move-beyond-law-into-practice)

**Liberia**
- The Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency (LDEA) adopted a new national action plan with a harm-reduction approach (prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, reintegration), developed with the Ministry of Health — [AllAfrica, Dec 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512190331.html)
- Only 163 people were receiving government-funded treatment: 37 inpatients in Grand Bassa and 126 outpatients in Congo Town. The figure comes from the Ministry of Health, and the article's date is unclear — [New Narratives](https://newnarratives.org/featured/were-failing-a-generation-experts-say-president-boakais-drug-war-lacks-urgency-as-generation-slips-through-the-cracks/)
- Enforcement: seizures fell from 5,186 kg in Q4 2025 to 422 kg in Q1 2026, including 115.66 kg of kush, with 233 arrests in Q1 2026 — [AllAfrica, Jun 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202606030334.html)

**Tanzania**
- Tanzania's first publicly funded methadone clinic opened in 2011 at Muhimbili National Hospital, with daily directly observed dosing — [PMC4435505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4435505)
- One report says the number of MAT clinics rose to 18, which have served 18,170 people cumulatively. The source's provenance is unclear and it likely dates from 2025 — [tanzania.eu.com](https://tanzania.eu.com/tanzania-governments-strong-actions-against-drug-problem). An older DCEA statement gives 7 clinics and 7,600 clients — [CND blog 2019](https://cndblog.org/2019/03/side-event-integrated-methadone-programme-with-take-away-doses-for-people-with-opioid-use-disorders-in-tanzania/)
- Temeke clinic (2025 study): more than 3,500 registered clients and about 960 active; about 12% of active clients receive take-away doses — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bjc/journl/v12y2025i6p1774-1778.html)
- At least one OAT site, Mbeya Zonal Referral Hospital, keeps treatment history and daily attendance in an electronic database. Tanzania also integrates HIV testing and ART into methadone clinics (the IMAT model) — [PMC4829437 / related](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4829437); [PMC4511823](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4511823/)

**Senegal**
- CEPIAD in Dakar opened in December 2014 as West Africa's first publicly funded OST programme. It had about 250 methadone patients in 2018 and around 2022, more than 75% of them men, with directly observed daily dosing — [PMC9128414](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9128414/); [Africanews 2022](https://africanews.com/2022/07/30/senegal-a-special-clinic-in-darkar-helps-patients-end-opioid-addiction/)

**Mauritius and Côte d'Ivoire**
- Mauritius is listed with Kenya and Tanzania among the few sub-Saharan countries with government-funded OST — [TalkingDrugs](https://www.talkingdrugs.org/senegal-leading-the-way-on-methadone-provision-in-west-africa/). I found no details on either country.

### Inferences
- Ghana shows that a public-health law alone changes little without regulations, a funded rehabilitation mechanism and training for police and judges. This matters for any review of Sierra Leone's 2008 Act.
- Tanzania and Senegal show that methadone programmes in low-income African settings are feasible. Their main data tool is a daily dosing register, electronic at some sites, which doubles as a retention tracker.

### Gaps
- I could not verify any biometric or fingerprint identification at Tanzanian MAT clinics. The searches returned no evidence for it, so treat the claim as unconfirmed.
- No current figures for Mauritius or Côte d'Ivoire. No current DCEA national client total.

## Q5. The West Africa Epidemiology Network on Drug Use (WENDU)

### Takeaway
WENDU is active, but its reports come out irregularly and with a long lag. The 2023 report was launched in Freetown on 11 September 2024 and highlighted kush as an emerging public health emergency. This suggests Sierra Leone takes part, but I could not find country-specific data.

### Cited Findings
- ECOWAS launched the 2023 WENDU report in Freetown on 11 September 2024. It covers drug supply, drug use and the availability of treatment, and flags kush as a public health emergency in several ECOWAS member states — [ECOWAS](https://www.ecowas.int/the-2023-west-african-epidemiology-network-on-drug-use-wendu-report-statistics-and-trends-on-illicit-drug-supply-and-drug-use-officially-released/)
- The accompanying policy brief, "Soaring Addiction, Lagging Response: West Africa's Creeping Drug Epidemic", was supported by ENACT and the EU — [ENACT/ISS](https://issafrica.org/enactafrica/events/drug-use-and-supply-in-west-africa-launch-of-2023-wendu-report)
- An earlier WENDU report covering 2018–2019 includes country sections — [ECOWAS PDF](https://ecowas.int/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/WENDU-REPORT-ENGLISH.pdf)

### Inferences
- Hosting the launch in Freetown, and the report's focus on kush, suggest Sierra Leone contributes data. The national epidemiology network that feeds WENDU likely depends on donor-funded data collection.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Sierra Leone's actual WENDU submissions or indicators, or whether a 2024 or 2025 WENDU report exists.

## Q6. Sierra Leone: current context, response, data and gaps

### Takeaway
Since the April 2024 emergency declaration, Sierra Leone has coordinated its response through the National Task Force on Drug and Substance Abuse (NaTFDSA), modelled on the COVID-19 coordination platform, under a five-pillar strategy. Treatment capacity has expanded quickly, from 50 to 212 beds in six centres, but follow-up data is weak (37% of discharged patients). Enforcement has hardened sharply since late 2025. The data system is fragmented across centres, and there is no evidence of a national patient registry.

### Cited Findings
- Kush was named in a 2024 Constitutional Instrument, with penalties of at least 15 years for its production and distribution — [SierraLII](https://sierralii.gov.sl/akn/sl/act/ci/2024/7/eng@2024-04-17/source.pdf)
- Composition: a GI-TOC study found nitazenes in more than 50% of samples and synthetic cannabinoids in the rest. Later testing reportedly confirmed nitazenes in 87% of samples — [GI-TOC](https://globalinitiative.net/announcements/new-report-on-kush-highlights-west-africas-growing-synthetic-drugs-challenge/); [AP via ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/health/2025/02/25/the-main-ingredients-in-sierra-leones-kush-are-synthetic-opioids-and-cannabinoids-report-finds/)
- UNODC: kush is the substance behind the largest number of people in drug treatment in Sierra Leone (World Drug Report 2025). Freetown kush samples from 2024 contained nitazenes and synthetic cannabinoids (World Drug Report 2026) — [WDR 2025](https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/WDR_2025/WDR25_B1_Key_findings.pdf); [WDR 2026 Highlights](https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/WDR_2026/WDR26_Highlights.pdf)
- Governance: NaTFDSA coordinates a five-pillar strategy and is modelled on the COVID-19 platform — [Sciety, preprint rs-7870467](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7870467/v1)
- Treatment capacity: one evaluation covering April 2024 to October 2025 reports beds rising from 50 to 212 across six centres (+324%), above a national target of 180. It counts 1,834 discharged patients, with follow-up data on employment and education for only 673 of them (37%). This is an unreviewed preprint — [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7870467/v1.pdf?c=1760721538000)
- Centres include:
  - The Sierra Leone Psychiatric Teaching Hospital drug rehabilitation centre (Kissy), opened June 2024, with Partners In Health (PIH) providing nursing support through November 2025–January 2026 — [PIH](https://www.pih.org/article/how-patients-are-healing-amid-sierra-leones-drug-crisis)
  - A second government detox centre on a military base in Bo, with 50 young men on a four-week programme as of mid-2025 — [eNCA/AFP](https://www.enca.com/opinion/addicts-fight-synthetic-kush-hell-sleone-rehab-centres)
  - A Caritas Freetown Kush Rehabilitation & Empowerment Centre — [ACI Africa](https://www.aciafrica.org/news/18847/caritas-freetown-launches-rehabilitation-centre-for-kush-addicts-in-sierra-leone)
- Informal "detox houses" chain people who cannot access care — [eNCA/AFP](https://www.enca.com/opinion/addicts-fight-synthetic-kush-hell-sleone-rehab-centres)
- Clinical surveillance: a cohort of 312 suspected kush poisonings at four treatment centres (June–November 2025): median age 24, 94% male, 14% severe outcomes, 9 deaths (3%). Risk factors were polysubstance use, previous overdose, presenting more than 6 hours after onset, and wound infection — [JIEPH/AFENET](https://afenet-journal.org/10-37432-jieph-d-25-00329/)
- In November 2025 the President announced a special drug court, a sentencing practice direction, a comprehensive review of drug legislation, a special police/military security unit, and a special fund for prevention and community engagement — [Sierra Leone Telegraph](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/president-bio-announces-five-new-measures-to-tackle-sierra-leones-kush-crisis/)
- The Chief Justice's practice directive, in force from 1 December 2025, sends offences under Sections 7(a)–(c) of the National Drugs Control Act 2008 to the High Court, with no bail and a reported 30-year minimum sentence. This comes from an advocacy source — [TalkingDrugs](https://www.talkingdrugs.org/30-year-minimum-sierra-leone-new-drug-laws-are-some-of-the-worlds-toughest/)
- Part IV of the 2008 Act already provides for treatment assessment panels, treatment orders and suspended penalties for offenders. Critics say criminalisation dominates and rehabilitation is under-resourced — [ManoReporters](https://manoreporters.com/news/health/from-criminalization-to-recoveryrethinking-sierra-leones-2008-national-drugs-control-act/); [SSHAP](https://www.socialscienceinaction.org/resources/sshap-west-africa-hub-addressing-the-kush-epidemic-in-sierra-leone/)
- The Sierra Leone Youth Drug Control League has called for a review of the 2008 Act in a policy brief to the new head of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) — [Calabash](https://thecalabashnewspaper.com/archives/64815)
- SSHAP characterises the response as largely punitive and data-poor — [SSHAP](https://www.socialscienceinaction.org/resources/sshap-west-africa-hub-addressing-the-kush-epidemic-in-sierra-leone/)
- WHO supported the kush response from April 2024 (headline only; specifics not retrieved) — [WHO AFRO](https://afro.who.int/countries/sierra-leone/publication/who-sierra-leone-releases-second-quarter-2026-results-newsletter)

### Inferences
- Sierra Leone has the ingredients for a system: multiple centres, a coordinating task force, clinical cohort studies and treatment provisions in the 2008 Act. What it lacks is the connective layer:
  - a common admission and discharge dataset across centres
  - a unique patient ID to deduplicate re-admissions
  - follow-up tracking after discharge
  - a referral pathway from police and courts into treatment.
- The 30-year minimum sentencing regime risks pushing users away from services, which makes confidentiality and data protection in any registry critical.

### Gaps
- No verified national total of people treated since 2024, and no verified budget for treatment or the task force.
- No confirmed list of all six centres with bed counts.
- No confirmation of a Ministry of Health and Sanitation (MoHS) digital system (for example DHIS2 indicators for substance use).
- No specifics found on EU or US INL funding for Sierra Leone's drug response.
- Unverified: the status of the legislative review and whether a new drug bill exists. I found no parliamentary vote.

## Q7. Lessons for Sierra Leone (synthesis across countries)

### Takeaway
Across the countries reviewed, the best-functioning parts are cheap and standards-based: an accreditation register (Kenya), a standard treatment-admission form with periodic aggregation (South Africa's SACENDU), daily dosing registers in OAT programmes (Tanzania, Senegal), a helpline tied to accredited referrals (Kenya's 1192) and a prevalence survey (Nigeria 2018). None of the systems found is a sophisticated national digital patient registry.

### Cited Findings
- Kenya: accreditation, monitoring and closure of non-compliant facilities, plus helpline referral only to accredited facilities — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/nacada-closes-15-rehabilitation-facilities-for-health-standards-violations-n375800); [NACADA](https://www.nacada.go.ke/node/290)
- South Africa: twice-yearly SACENDU treatment-admission trends — [SAMRC](https://www.samrc.ac.za/sites/default/files/attachments/2026-09/Sacendu%20Research%20Update%20Jul-Dec%202025.pdf)
- Integrated harm reduction and OST within primary care (COSUP), and HIV services integrated into methadone clinics (Tanzania IMAT) — [PMC7284158](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7284158/); [PMC4511823](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4511823/)
- Ghana: a public-health law stalls without regulations and a funded rehabilitation mechanism — [GNA](https://gna.org.gh/2026/08/nacoc-presses-government-for-rehabilitation-fund/)
- Sierra Leone already has a 63% follow-up gap among discharged patients — [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7870467/v1.pdf?c=1760721538000)

### Inferences
- Suggested priorities for a Sierra Leone drug-user management system, such as a Mobi-Care module:
  1. A minimum dataset aligned with SACENDU/WENDU indicators: demographics, primary substance, route, previous treatment, referral source, HIV/TB status and outcomes.
  2. A unique patient ID that works across centres, to track re-admission and relapse.
  3. Mobile follow-up at 1, 3 and 6 months after discharge, aimed at the 63% follow-up gap.
  4. A register of accredited facilities and standards, à la NACADA, to bring informal detox houses under regulation.
  5. A helpline and referral workflow, including court- and police-initiated referrals under the treatment-order provisions in Part IV of the 2008 Act.
  6. Overdose and poisoning surveillance linked to emergency departments, building on the JIEPH cohort design.
  7. Strong confidentiality and role-based access, given the punitive sentencing context.
  8. Readiness for OAT dosing registers if methadone or buprenorphine is introduced for nitazene dependence.
- These recommendations are my synthesis, not sourced claims.

### Gaps
- No African country was found with a published, fully national, interoperable electronic drug-treatment patient registry to use as a direct template. This is worth confirming with UNODC's treatment-data guidance (e.g., the UNODC/WHO International Standards and the Treatment Demand Indicator protocol), which I did not retrieve here.
