## 1. What system did the Sierra Leone NDLEA head likely see, and which body runs it?

### Takeaway
I found **no public report** of a Sierra Leone NDLEA visit to Botswana. I also found **no named digital drug-user registry or case-management system** in Botswana. The most likely "system" is the **Botswana Drug Enforcement Agency (DEA)-led multi-sectoral model**:
- The legal basis is the 2018 Act.
- It is coordinated through the National Drug Control Master Strategy and its Monitoring & Evaluation Framework.
- The DEA has an Education and Outreach / rehabilitation mandate and treats people who use drugs "as patients, not criminals".
- Clinical treatment runs through the Ministry of Health: Sbrana Psychiatric Hospital, Jubilee Hospital and the new Serowe (old Sekgoma Memorial Hospital) Drug & Alcohol Rehabilitation Centre.
- The model is aligned to the UNODC/WHO International Standards for the Treatment of Drug Use Disorders.

This is an inference, not a confirmed answer.

## 2. Legal basis

### Takeaway
The DEA and rehab centres rest on the **Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 2018** (in force 29 June 2018). The name used in the brief, "Narcotics, Psychotropic Substances and Precursor Chemicals Act 2018", appears to be a misnomer. The Act has a Part on rehabilitation centres. I found no confirmed court or police diversion-to-treatment mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- Official title, commencement 29 June 2018, a Part on rehabilitation centres (s.26 in the table of contents), and a precursor-chemical definition by reference to the 1988 UN Convention — [BotswanaLaws consolidated statute](https://botswanalaws.com/consolidated-statutes/principle-legislation/illicit-traffic-in-narcotic-drugs-and-psychotropic-substances)
- INCB: the Act provides the legal basis for rehab centres and the DEA — [INCB AR 2018](https://www.incb.org/incb/uploads/documents/Publications/AnnualReports/AR2018/Annual_Report_Chapters/06_Chapter_III_Africa_Annual_Report_2018_E_.pdf)
- A search summary of a 2025/26 Daily News item said that **amendments** to the Act added provisions for rehabilitation and reintegration, with "victims" referred to care centres — [Daily News 89237](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/89237). I could not read the full text. It is unclear whether this is a new amendment or a description of the 2018 provisions.
- The Medicines and Related Substances Act 2025 (BoMRA) regulates licit controlled medicines. It is not part of the user-management system — [BoMRA PDF](https://www.bomra.co.bw/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Medicines-and-Related-Substances-Act-2025-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- If the reported amendment exists, it may create a statutory referral pathway from enforcement to care centres. That would be the closest thing to "diversion".

### Gaps
- Text of s.26 and of any amendment; whether courts can impose treatment orders; whether possession for personal use is still prosecuted as standard.

## 3. Workflow: identification, referral, assessment, treatment, rehabilitation, reintegration, follow-up

### Takeaway
The pathway is only partly documented:
- **Identification** comes through DEA/police enforcement, drug testing, family (parent) requests and community/school outreach.
- **Treatment** has historically been at Sbrana Psychiatric Hospital (Lobatse) and Jubilee Hospital (Francistown).
- **Inpatient rehab** is at the new Serowe centre.
- **Counselling** comes from NGOs (BOSASNET, BOCAIP, private hubs).

Formal assessment protocols, reintegration and follow-up are stated as policy goals. I found no documentation of how they operate.

### Cited Findings
- **Family-driven entry:** DG Ramakorwane said more parents are asking the DEA to admit their children to rehabilitation — [Mmegi, "Drug abuse rampant, parents request more rehabs – DEA"](https://www.mmegi.bw/news/drug-abuse-rampant-parents-request-more-rehabs-dea/news)
- **Historic treatment:** Botswana relied mainly on Sbrana Psychiatric Hospital and Jubilee Hospital for substance treatment — [Daily News 88546](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/88546)
- **Serowe centre (Ministry of Health), the country's first dedicated public drug and alcohol rehab:**
  - It is at the old Sekgoma Memorial Hospital, with 50 beds (30 adult, 20 adolescent).
  - It offers free inpatient and outpatient care, detox and aftercare.
  - Phased opening was set for November 2025, after the original May 2022 completion target slipped — [ISSUP Botswana, Aug 2025](https://www.issup.net/national-chapters/issup-botswana/news/2025-08/sekgoma-memorial-hospital-drug-alcohol-rehab-centre-set-open-soon); [Gabz FM](https://www.gabzfm.com/post/new-in-patient-rehabilitation-centre-to-open-in-serowe); [Daily News 88546 "rehabilitation centre opens November"](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/88546)
  - A centre had been promised since the alcohol levy was introduced under President Khama, nearly a decade earlier (2017 article) — [Sunday Standard](https://www.sundaystandard.info/national-alcohol-and-drug-rehabilitation-centre-to-be-built-in-serowe/)
- **More centres:** A 2026 World Drug Day report said the government committed **P80 million** to build rehabilitation centres in **Lobatse and Maun**, with Serowe "nearing completion" — [ISSUP node 70139, 2026 World Drug Day](https://www.issup.net/node/70139). In July 2026 UNODC welcomed "two rehabilitation centres" as a milestone — [Mmegi 123238](https://www.mmegi.bw/ampArticle/123238). **Conflict:** another search found no evidence of built government centres in Lobatse or Maun, only residents' requests in Maun.
- **Standards:** In July 2026 the DEA hosted a UNODC workshop on the International Standards for the Treatment of Drug Use Disorders. UNODC urged a comprehensive, evidence-based treatment system and pledged support, including for community-based treatment — [Mmegi 123238](https://www.mmegi.bw/ampArticle/123238); [Daily News 92160](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/92160)
- **NGO counselling:** National policy recommends counselling, but it is generally unavailable in primary care. NGOs such as BOSASNET provide much of the outpatient counselling. This is from an older UNDP desk review (c. 2017) — [UNDP desk review of the National Alcohol Policy](https://info.undp.org/docs/pdc/Documents/BWA/5383%20UNDP%20DESK%20REVIEW%20OF%20THE%20BOTSWANA%20NATIONAL%20ALCOHOL%20POLICY%20TEXT.pdf). BOCAIP and Sbrana are listed as free counselling contacts (2019) — [Twitter/X resource list](https://twitter.com/TheAnswerKarabo/status/1188053685571600386). BOCAIP is mainly an HIV/counselling NGO — [US State Dept COP 2007](https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Botswana-4.pdf)
- **Private providers:** Onalenna Rehabilitation Hub calls itself Botswana's first free, evidence-based rehab centre (self-description) — [onalenna.org](https://www.onalenna.org/)
- **Relapse prevention / reintegration:** An academic study proposes a multidisciplinary youth relapse-prevention programme involving police, families and services. It is a proposal, not an operating system — [HSAG journal article](https://hsag.co.za/index.php/hsag/article/view/2965/6088)
- **Enforcement side:** P41 million was again budgeted for DEA drug testing and detection equipment — [Mmegi](https://www.mmegi.bw/news/dea-to-purchase-drug-testing-detection-equipment/news). A six-month narcotics investigation course with the Botswana Police Service at Otse Police College was opened in 2026 — [Mmegi via search](https://www.mmegi.bw/ampArticle/123238). There were 2,650 drug-related cases over two years (older Daily News report) — [Daily News 78615](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/78615)
- **Capacity:** The government is expanding the DEA with more staff, detection dogs and transport — [ISSUP node 70139](https://www.issup.net/node/70139)

### Inferences
- Likely flow: arrest, drug test or parental/self referral, then the DEA (education/rehab directorate) or a health facility, then psychiatric/clinical assessment (Sbrana/Jubilee/Serowe), then in/outpatient treatment, then NGO counselling and aftercare. This chain is reconstructed from scattered sources and is not a documented SOP.

### Gaps
- Formal screening/assessment tools (e.g., ASSIST), referral forms, court diversion, and the reintegration or follow-up mechanism (social workers, Ministry of Youth programmes) were not found.
- Whether Serowe actually opened, and its admission numbers, could not be confirmed. Reports conflict between "opens November" (2025) and later "nearing completion" (2026).

## 4. Digital platform, data captured, access and privacy

### Takeaway
I found **no evidence of a named national drug-user registry, case-management system or demand-reduction information system** in Botswana. What exists is:
- an M&E Framework attached to the Master Strategy;
- participation in the AU's Pan-African Epidemiology Network on Drug Use (PAENDU), which implies a national drug epidemiology network or observatory;
- general Ministry of Health EMR systems (PIMS2/IPMS), which are not drug-specific.

Any such data would fall under the **Data Protection Act 18 of 2024**.

### Cited Findings
- As of 31 Dec 2021, Botswana was among 32 countries whose national drug epidemiology networks were linked to PAENDU. That model is a national observatory or expert network monitoring trends from existing data sources — [JIED, LSE](https://jied.lse.ac.uk/articles/10.31389/jied.179)
- UNODC supports integrated drug information systems and national observatories (general, not Botswana-specific) — [UNODC drug use page](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/data-and-analysis/drug-use-new.html)
- The Ministry of Health uses PIMS2/IPMS electronic patient records (HIV and maternal care context) — [Botswana Combination Prevention Project protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/70/NCT01965470/Prot_001.pdf)
- **Data Protection Act 18 of 2024:**
  - Assented 19 Aug 2024. Commencement is given as 29 Oct 2024 by DLA Piper but as 14 Jan 2025 by IFEX/MISA (conflict).
  - It replaced the 2018 Act, which never commenced.
  - It has rules on sensitive personal data, mandatory DPIAs for high-risk processing and breach-notification timelines.
  - It creates an independent Information and Data Protection Commission with search/seizure powers — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=BW&t=law); [IFEX](https://ifex.org/?p=353078); [CIPIT](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/understanding-botswanas-2018-and-2024-data-protection-acts/); [Michalsons Bill PDF](https://www.michalsons.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Botswana-Data-Protection-Bill-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- A drug-user registry would almost certainly be "high-risk processing" of sensitive (health/criminal) data. It would need a DPIA and a lawful basis under the 2024 Act. Whether health data is in the statutory "sensitive" list is not confirmed from snippets.
- The visitor may have been shown the M&E framework dashboards or DEA case-tracking. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- No information on what data the DEA or the Serowe centre records, who can access it, or links between enforcement and health records.

### Takeaway
2025–2026 shows growing investment:
- the Serowe centre;
- a reported P80m for Lobatse and Maun centres;

- DEA staffing;

- Botswana's appeal to a peer agency is probably the model of a **single statutory drug agency combining enforcement with education and rehabilitation, backed by a costed national strategy and an M&E framework**. A sophisticated digital system is unlikely to be the draw.
